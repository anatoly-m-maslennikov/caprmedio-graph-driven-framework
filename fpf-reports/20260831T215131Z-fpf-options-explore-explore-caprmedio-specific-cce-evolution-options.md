4. **Runtime verifiability:** deterministic coverage, reproducibility, and diagnostic boundaries.
5. **Migration burden:** authority changes, corpus rewrites, tooling, and training required.

#### O1 — Authority Closure

- **Purpose:** Make the current governed authoring convention complete enough to version, apply, and audit without claiming formal parsing.
- **Architecture:** Add only missing active authority for `cce_1`, forms, profiles, ownership, and evaluation bindings. Retain human-authored Claim text as authority and current projections as derived.
- **Mechanism lineage:** Current CCE plus M7, M8, M11, and the authority parts of M13.
- **Benefits:** Highest fit to current structure; smallest conceptual change; low authority-contamination risk; creates prerequisites for every stronger option.
- **Limits and failure modes:** “One precise interpretation” remains human-validated. A complete registry can create false confidence if runtime conformance remains absent.
- **Cost / reversibility:** `M` cost; high reversibility because it completes governance rather than replacing the Claim representation.
- **Preconditions:** Owner disposition through CA-P-037 or its current successor; exact inventory of forms and their active uses.
- **Evidence needed:** Registry completeness, no orphan forms, migration dry run, direct Evaluation coverage, and proof that no tool is treated as interpretation authority.
- **Coordinate profile:** Integrity very high; semantic assurance current; usability high; runtime verifiability low-to-medium; migration burden low; reversibility high; provenance independence high.
- **Candidate-definition confidence:** 99%. Feasibility and acceptance remain undecided.

#### O2 — Deterministic Authoring Check

- **Purpose:** Give authors reproducible feedback for the mechanically decidable subset without claiming full semantic understanding.
- **Architecture:** `F0` plus a stateless checker for metadata, canonical Terms and Operators, Claim/Scope cardinality, prohibited ambiguity patterns, explicit fields required by active Methods, and projection lineage. Every diagnostic declares `error`, `warning`, or `unknown` and cites active authority.
- **Mechanism lineage:** O1 plus M4, M6, M10, and M13.
- **Benefits:** Fast feedback; executable Evaluations; high replayability; no need to define a whole-language parser first.
- **Limits and failure modes:** Surface checks can produce false positives and false negatives. Passing cannot prove one interpretation, truth, or safety. Gate use before calibration could block valid Claims.
- **Cost / reversibility:** `M–L` cost; high reversibility if the checker remains a replaceable Tool and receipts retain authority editions.
- **Preconditions:** O1 registry closure, diagnostic taxonomy, original fixtures, and an explicit advisory/gating policy.
- **Evidence needed:** Precision/recall by diagnostic class, false-block rate, replayability, unchanged-Claim checks, and static-versus-human disagreement records.
- **Coordinate profile:** Integrity high; semantic assurance low-to-medium; usability medium-to-high; runtime verifiability medium; migration burden low-to-medium; reversibility high; provenance independence high.
- **Candidate-definition confidence:** 99%. Operational value remains unmeasured.

#### O3 — Semantic Signature Projection

- **Purpose:** Make the semantic facets already required by `CA-M-113` visible to graph tools without first solving full natural-language parsing.
- **Architecture:** `F0` plus a derived, explicitly non-authoritative Claim Signature containing participants, relation/predicate, modality, quantity, condition, boundary, and grouping. The complete Claim and Claim Scope remain authority. The signature carries source lineage and can be regenerated or discarded.
- **Mechanism lineage:** O1 plus M3–M6 and CAPRMEDIO’s current graph/projection model.
- **Benefits:** Graph queries and diffs can inspect required semantic facets; the projection can expose omissions; migration can be incremental.
- **Limits and failure modes:** A manually authored signature risks becoming a second authority. A machine-derived signature cannot be trusted without sufficient parsing. A human-confirmed signature is evidence, not proof that every wording maps uniquely.
- **Cost / reversibility:** `M–L` cost; high reversibility only while the signature stays derived and disposable.
- **Preconditions:** Exact projection status, no reconstruction from summaries, source-currentness checks, and a policy for unresolved extraction.
- **Evidence needed:** Regeneration determinism, Claim-to-signature agreement studies, stale-signature detection, and tests proving that deleting the signature loses no authority.
- **Coordinate profile:** Integrity medium-to-high; semantic assurance medium; usability medium; runtime verifiability medium; migration burden medium; reversibility high if strictly derived; provenance independence high.
- **Candidate-definition confidence:** 97%. Whether the signature adds enough value without duplicating meaning is unresolved.

#### O4 — Role-Profiled Formalization

- **Purpose:** Formalize CCE incrementally by Content Role or registered `cce_form`, while sharing one term and Operator kernel.
- **Architecture:** `F0` plus a registry of form profiles. Each admitted profile has its own grammar fragment, allowed Operators, semantic mapping, canonical examples, and conformance suite. A form is called parseable only after its profile passes. Unformalized forms remain explicitly convention-only rather than silently inheriting formal status.
- **Mechanism lineage:** O1 plus M1–M5, M8, M11, and M13; it uses the observed form diversity rather than imposing one immediate grammar.
- **Benefits:** Bounded rollout; smaller grammars; role-specific expressiveness; failures are localized; current 28-form usage becomes governable.
- **Limits and failure modes:** The project can temporarily contain mixed conformance levels. Profile drift can produce different meanings for shared Operators. Cross-profile Claims and migrations need explicit bridge rules.
- **Cost / reversibility:** `L` cost; medium reversibility because admitted profiles and migrated Claims create dependencies, but rollout can stop at a form boundary.
- **Preconditions:** O1, profile precedence, shared semantic kernel, role/form inventory, and a no-silent-fallback rule.
- **Evidence needed:** Per-profile parse/render tests, shared-Operator invariants, cross-profile interaction tests, migration receipts, and conformance status visible on every affected Claim.
- **Coordinate profile:** Integrity high with strict registry discipline; semantic assurance high for admitted profiles and current elsewhere; usability medium; runtime verifiability high within covered profiles; migration burden medium-to-high; reversibility medium; provenance independence high.
- **Candidate-definition confidence:** 98%. The correct profile boundary is unresolved.

#### O5 — Unified Formal CCE Compiler

- **Purpose:** Make every authoritative CCE Claim deterministically parseable to one typed meaning and canonically renderable.
- **Architecture:** `F0` plus one closed/versioned grammar, typed intermediate representation, deterministic parser, canonical renderer, graph projection, fail-closed diagnostics, and round-trip conformance. Claim text remains the authoritative carrier; the typed representation is a source-derived interpretation unless the owner explicitly changes that boundary.
- **Mechanism lineage:** The strongest completion of M1–M8, M10, M11, and M13, consistent with the archived formal-design direction but requiring new active disposition.
- **Benefits:** Strongest machine-verifiable interpretation; canonicalization; robust diffs; executable semantic Evaluations; one common conformance frontier.
- **Limits and failure modes:** Highest design and migration cost; expressiveness may shrink; grammar mistakes can systematize wrong interpretations; large migrations can damage project integrity; renderer output can be mistaken for new authority.
- **Cost / reversibility:** `XL` cost; low-to-medium reversibility after authoritative corpus migration, high only during isolated prototyping.
- **Preconditions:** Explicit primary-purpose decision, O1, semantic target, complete grammar and Operator semantics, corpus coverage, migration rollback, and owner acceptance of formal-language trade-offs.
- **Evidence needed:** Total parse coverage for the admitted corpus, one-meaning tests, parse/render/parse equality, semantic graph equivalence, negative/adversarial fixtures, performance, reviewer comprehension, and lossless rollback.
- **Coordinate profile:** Integrity potentially very high but migration-sensitive; semantic assurance highest; usability initially low-to-medium; runtime verifiability highest; migration burden highest; reversibility lowest after adoption; provenance independence high.
- **Candidate-definition confidence:** 99%. Implementability and proportionality remain unproved.

#### O6 — AI-Assisted Authoring Overlay

- **Purpose:** Help humans draft or repair Claims using the active CCE authority while leaving validation and acceptance elsewhere.
- **Architecture:** `F0` plus retrieved authority context, constrained generation, author-time suggestions, deterministic post-checks where available, provenance receipts, and explicit human acceptance. The AI layer is a replaceable host adapter, not CCE authority and not a conformance oracle.
- **Mechanism lineage:** M6, M9, M10, and the Chelebi-inspired layered-feedback hypothesis from Call 2. No external prompt, skill, hook, linter, or code is reused.
- **Benefits:** Potentially lower author effort; can explain diagnostics; easy to experiment with across hosts; highly reversible.
- **Limits and failure modes:** Nondeterminism, plausible but wrong rewrites, authority leakage into prompts, sensitive-data exposure, model drift, and proxy optimization. It cannot close CCE-GAP-001–003 by itself.
- **Cost / reversibility:** `M` cost for a governed prototype; high reversibility if receipts and adapters are isolated.
- **Preconditions:** At least O1; preferably O2 for deterministic checking; data policy; model/version logging; human acceptance; and no generated authority mutation without normal governance.
- **Evidence needed:** Meaning-preservation review, hallucination and omission rates, provenance completeness, model/version replay limits, leakage tests, edit savings, and comparison with deterministic-only authoring.
- **Coordinate profile:** Integrity medium unless tightly isolated; semantic assurance not improved by itself; usability potentially high but unproved; runtime verifiability low for generation and inherited from deterministic checks; migration burden low; reversibility high; provenance independence high if independently implemented.
- **Candidate-definition confidence:** 99%. Benefit for CCE remains experimental.

### 4. Declared-coordinate evaluation and diversity map

No empirical Pareto front is justified yet. The candidates are structurally non-equivalent and remain retained for different reasons:

- **O1** occupies the minimum-change frontier: strongest current fit and low burden, but it does not materially increase machine semantic assurance.
- **O2** occupies the deterministic-feedback frontier: better executable coverage than O1 without the cost of a formal language, but it cannot prove meaning.
- **O3** occupies the graph-visibility frontier: it exposes semantic facets without full parsing, but carries the greatest risk of a shadow representation.
- **O4** occupies the staged-formalization frontier: stronger semantics with bounded rollout, but mixed conformance and profile drift become new governance problems.
- **O5** occupies the maximum-formalization frontier: strongest semantic and runtime objectives, with the largest cost, migration, and reversibility burden.
- **O6** occupies the assistance frontier: potentially improves authoring experience and remains replaceable, but it is not a standalone CCE-completion option.

The primary diversity is architectural, not lexical: O1 governs prose, O2 checks prose, O3 derives a semantic view, O4 formalizes role-by-role, O5 compiles the whole language, and O6 assists the author. **Confidence: 99%.**

The following dominance claims are explicitly **not** made:

- O5 is not “best” merely because it is most formal.
- O1 is not “best” merely because it is cheapest.
- O6 is not equivalent to deterministic validation.
- O3’s structured fields do not automatically have higher authority than prose.
- O4’s incremental rollout does not automatically cost less overall than O5.
