# Semantic irreducibility

Every Atom contains only content necessary to preserve its role-specific atomic
unit. A statement remains only when removing it would cause material semantic
or operational loss to that unit's meaning, scope, applicability, boundary,
falsifiability, lifecycle, or required use.

An Atom must not create a second authoritative owner for a governed meaning. It
uses the exact canonical term and a direct relation to the owning Atom instead
of independently restating another definition or Claim. A minimal explanatory
gloss or recognition statement may repeat enough wording for the intended
reader only when it preserves the canonical owner's identity and meaning and
introduces no independent authority. Rationale belongs to Analysis, and an
example remains only when necessary to bound or disambiguate the atomic unit.

Required carrier identity and navigation labels are not semantic content and
are derived from their canonical owner wherever possible. Historical repetition
preserved in immutable revisions or archived Atoms does not create a second
current owner.
